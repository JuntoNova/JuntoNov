A stake in the dream

The American Dream works best when every citizen owns a real stake in the country's future.

When household costs stay too high, ownership slips out of reach. People stop building and start renting just to survive. And when Americans cannot own their future, they begin to lose their connection to America, to its key institutions, and ultimately to themselves.

Junto Nova finds what keeps those costs high and does the work that lowers prices. You can hire Junto Nova and keep every effort proprietary. Published lessons make the whole field smarter and more cost-effective, and raise the odds of a future every American can own.

Your share of America

We live in the richest country in history, at the best time in the world's history. Yet the average American household's share of just the federal debt is nearly negative $300,000. And a record 55% of Americans say their financial situation is getting worse.

People can feel that pocketbook pain. It shows up as less ownership, less confidence, and less trust in the institutions that are supposed to make a stake possible.

About us

In 1727, a young Benjamin Franklin gathered a dozen tradesmen and artisans in Philadelphia into a club for mutual improvement. They called it the Junto. Outsiders called it the Leather Apron Club, because the members were the kind of people who wore leather aprons to work: printers, surveyors, cabinetmakers, clerks. Not a debating society for gentlemen. A working group that asked hard questions, shared knowledge, and then built things.

Out of that small circle came the first subscription library in America, the first fire insurance company, a volunteer fire department, a hospital, a college, and habits of practical problem-solving that shaped the early republic. They did not wait for permission. They put on the apron and got to work.

That is exactly the kind of American effort we still need. Not just thinkers. People willing to dig into the barriers that keep costs high, build the organizations and deals that lower those costs, and stay long enough for the proof to stick.

Nova means a new start. Junto Nova is a new Junto: practical people, focused on ownership and lower costs, ready to put on the leather apron.

People who will put money, a plan, or operating muscle behind a future cheap enough to own and build.

Three beliefs shape how we work. Each will get a full note on Substack. Until then, the short version:

1. Good policy is not enough.

It has to be good politics too, or it does not stick. Sustainable reform needs coalitions, timing, and a path that can survive the next election cycle, not just the next white paper.

2. Measures matter, but only the ones people feel.

What households care about most is cost. Rent, tuition, the power bill, the doctor's bill, the tax bill. If the metric does not show up in daily life, it will not drive ownership or trust.

3. Proof beats process.

Strategy decks are cheap. The work is building the organization, running the campaign, and proving the model in a few places until scale starts to become inevitable. We stay for that part.
