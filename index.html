A stake in the dream

The American Dream works best when every citizen owns a real stake in the country's future.

When household costs stay too high, ownership slips out of reach. People stop building and start renting just to survive. And when Americans cannot own their future, they begin to lose their connection to America, to its key institutions, and ultimately to themselves.

The way back is more money in a family's pocket: lower bills, bigger paychecks, and savings that grow. If a policy doesn't help the family budget, it doesn't count.

Some rules raise your bills and shrink your paycheck. We show you how much, then work to change them so families keep more and own more.

About us

In 1727, a young Benjamin Franklin gathered about a dozen tradesmen and artisans in Philadelphia into a club for mutual improvement. They called it the Junto, also known as the Leather Apron Club, a name for people who worked with their hands. Its members were mostly tradesmen and artisans: printers, surveyors, a joiner, clerks. More than a debating society. A working group that asked hard questions, shared knowledge, and then built things.

Out of that circle came America's first successful lending library, a volunteer fire company, and an academy that became the University of Pennsylvania. Franklin went on to help found a hospital with Dr. Thomas Bond and a fire insurance company with his fellow volunteer firefighters. They did not wait for permission. They put on the apron and got to work.

That is exactly the kind of American effort we still need. Not just thinkers. People willing to dig into the rules that keep bills high and paychecks small, build the organizations and deals that fix both, and stay long enough for the proof to stick.

Nova is Latin for new. Junto Nova is a new Junto: practical people, focused on ownership and lower costs, ready to put on the leather apron.
