A stake in the dream

The American Dream works best when every citizen owns a real stake in the country's future.

When household costs stay too high, ownership slips out of reach. People stop building and start renting just to survive. And when Americans cannot own their future, they begin to lose their connection to America, to its key institutions, and ultimately to themselves.

Junto Nova reveals what keeps those costs high and does the work that lowers prices. You can hire Junto Nova and keep your work proprietary. If you let the key insights and lessons be publicly released, no one else will need to pay to learn the same lessons later.

Publicly available research and insights make the whole market smarter and more cost-effective, and raise the odds of a future every American can own.

About us

In 1727, a young Benjamin Franklin gathered about a dozen tradesmen and artisans in Philadelphia into a club for mutual improvement. They called it the Junto. It was also known as the Leather Apron Club, a name for people who worked with their hands. Its members were mostly tradesmen and artisans: printers, surveyors, a joiner, clerks. More than a debating society. A working group that asked hard questions, shared knowledge, and then built things.

Out of that circle came America's first successful lending library, a volunteer fire company, and an academy that became the University of Pennsylvania. Franklin went on to help found a hospital with Dr. Thomas Bond and a fire insurance company with his fellow volunteer firefighters. They did not wait for permission. They put on the apron and got to work.

That is exactly the kind of American effort we still need. Not just thinkers. People willing to dig into the barriers that keep costs high, build the organizations and deals that lower those costs, and stay long enough for the proof to stick.

Nova is Latin for new. Junto Nova is a new Junto: practical people, focused on ownership and lower costs, ready to put on the leather apron.
