Build the strategy

A plan with numbers, targets, and a clear path to capital or political support — mapped across the same continuum that diagnosis revealed.

Most strategies never become real

They stop at vision statements, white papers, or slide decks that list “reform housing” or “expand school choice.” They never answer the only questions that matter:

What is the specific cost reduction we are buying (in dollars per household, not abstract percentages)? What public support band actually exists for that reduction? Which political actors can carry it past the next election? Where is capital already flowing or blocked, and what term structure unlocks it? What is the first measurable proof that forces the rest of the continuum to move?

A strategy that does not sit on the continuum from cost-raising to cost-lowering policy is just process. We build the other kind.

What we build

01 · TARGET & MATH

The number that has to move

Clear household-level cost targets (rent, tuition, energy, medical, transport, taxes) with baselines, high/low paths, and the delta that constitutes a real win. No vague “affordability.” Actual dollars.

02 · SUPPORT BAND

What the public will accept and fight for

Translation of the diagnosis’s public-opinion layer into the precise policy window that can stick. Intensity, not just direction. The band that separates theater from durable reform.

03 · POLITICAL PATH

Who carries it and what they gain

Incentive-aligned sequence of legislative or regulatory moves, whip counts, champion identification, and the minimum coalition that survives the next cycle. Policy that cannot clear an election is not a strategy.

04 · CAPITAL & DEAL STRUCTURE

The money that makes the model real

Term sheets, vehicle design, funder sequencing, and the proof points capital requires before it commits. Strategy without a funded path is a memo.

05 · FIRST PROOF

The smallest win that changes the continuum

The pilot, model, or legislative foothold that demonstrates lower costs and expands ownership. Designed so success forces the next political and capital moves rather than waiting for perfect conditions.

Why numbers and the continuum matter

Strategy without targets is aspiration. Targets without a support band and political path are academic. Capital without a clear first proof stays on the sidelines. Most cost-of-living efforts fail because they treat these as sequential afterthoughts instead of a single map.

In housing, for example, pure deregulation often sits outside the current public support band. Status-quo density rules sit deep in the cost-raising zone. The workable strategy only appears when you place both on the continuum, set a concrete household rent/ownership target, identify the political actors who can expand supply inside the band, structure the capital that builds the first units, and design the proof that forces the next jurisdiction to follow. That is the difference between a plan that is discussed and a plan that lowers costs.
