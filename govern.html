Govern the scale

Board seats, board management, meetings and decks, external audits, and ongoing consulting so the model that lowered costs keeps doing it - and does not drift back into the process that raised them.

Scale without oversight is how wins reverse

The first wins are fragile. New supply gets captured. A reform statute gets reinterpreted. An operating model softens under board drift, staff turnover, or donor pressure that prefers activity over cost outcomes.

Governing the scale means staying in the room where those decisions get made - or giving the people in that room the cadence, materials, and independent pressure they need to hold the line. The job is not more process. It is ensuring scale happens without backsliding.

Ongoing consulting

Not every engagement needs a formal seat. Sometimes the right move is retained advisory: a regular cadence with leadership and the board to pressure-test decisions, flag backsliding risk, and keep the strategy honest as conditions change.

That can sit alongside board management and materials, or stand alone when the organization already has a functioning board but needs an independent voice on cost outcomes.

How you buy this

Start with the form of oversight that matches the risk. A seat when the model is critical and the room needs a permanent defender of the outcome. Management and materials when the board is willing but under-structured. Audits and consulting when independent pressure is the missing piece.

Why stay after the first wins

Most cost-lowering work celebrates the pilot and moves on. Then the board softens the standard, staff optimizes for funding over cost outcomes, and the statute or model that worked gets reinterpreted until it no longer does.

Governing the scale is the unglamorous half of the work: same metrics, same mandate, same refusal to trade ownership for process. Whether that is a seat, a managed board, an audit, or retained consulting - the point is the same. Scale without backsliding.
