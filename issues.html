What helps Americans own their future

Why change is hard

Owning your future takes two things: lower bills and bigger paychecks. Rules get in the way of both.

To change a rule, three things have to move: what people believe, the law itself, and what families actually see in their budget. The grid shows where each issue stands.

For example: minimum lot sizes

Cultural
Families should be able to buy the kind of home that's best for them.
Structural
End minimum lot size rules.
Practical
In Houston, the median new small lot townhouse sold for about $205,000 less than the median of other new homes. The two are different kinds of homes, not one home on two lot sizes.
How to use the grid

Pick an issue and a level above the grid. Each dot is one action you can take on that issue. The lens decides what kind:

  • Cultural: a value to stand for and build your message around.
  • Structural: a rule to change, as a specific proposal.
  • Practical: a change that moves the money now, like building homes on smaller lots where cities allow them.

Higher means more money at stake for families. Further right means the change is more likely to happen. Click a dot to see the evidence, how sure we are, and the values, rules and practical changes tied to that action.
